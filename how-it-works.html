How it works

From first conversation to a working system

A structured, transparent process that starts with your business — not the technology. You'll know exactly what is being built, why, and what it will cost before any work begins.

Start with a consultation
01
Step 1 · Discovery

Understand your existing business processes.

  • A conversation about how your business runs today
  • Which tools you use and where time is being lost
  • Your goals and any constraints (budget, data, team)
02
Step 2 · Assessment

Identify repetitive tasks suitable for automation.

  • Map the tasks that are repetitive and rule-based
  • Estimate where automation will have real impact
  • Flag anything that should stay manual
03
Step 3 · Design

Develop a customised automation solution.

  • A written solution design with workflow diagram
  • Clear scope, timeline and quote
  • Agreement on approvals, data and access
04
Step 4 · Implementation

Connect the necessary applications.

  • Build the workflows in Make.com
  • Connect Gmail, Google Sheets, Calendar and other tools
  • Configure AI instructions for your business
05
Step 5 · Testing

Verify that workflows function correctly.

  • Test with realistic scenarios and edge cases
  • Check error handling and notifications
  • Your review and sign-off before going live
06
Step 6 · Handover

Train the client and provide documentation.

  • A walkthrough training session
  • Simple documentation and how-to guides
  • Clear ownership of accounts and access
07
Step 7 · Ongoing support

Monitor and improve the system.

  • Optional monthly maintenance
  • Monitoring, fixes and adjustments
  • Improvements as your business changes
What I need from you

Simple to get started

  • Time for a discovery conversation (usually 30–60 minutes)
  • Examples of the tasks or emails you want to automate
  • Access to the relevant accounts, granted securely when we build
  • Feedback during testing
Principles

How I work

  • Practical first — automate what genuinely saves time
  • Human in the loop — approvals where decisions matter
  • Transparent costs — including third-party tool costs
  • Your accounts, your data — systems run in tools you own
  • Documented — you understand what was built
